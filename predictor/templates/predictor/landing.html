Numerical methods, explained
Every technique has a mathematical foundation. Here's what powers the engine.
METHOD 01
Bisection Method
Root-finding O(log n)
Divides an interval [a, b] repeatedly, halving the search space each iteration. Guaranteed to converge when f(a)·f(b) < 0. Used to find break-even points where Profit(x) = 0.
c = (a + b) / 2 → shrink interval containing root
METHOD 02
Newton-Raphson
Root-finding Quadratic convergence
Uses the tangent line at each iterate to find the next approximation. Quadratically faster than Bisection — errors roughly square at each step. Requires f'(x) ≠ 0.
x_{n+1} = x_n - f(x_n) / f'(x_n)
METHOD 03
Linear Regression
Least squares Forecasting
Fits y = mx + b to minimize sum of squared residuals. R² measures goodness of fit (1.0 = perfect). Used for revenue trend detection and multi-period forecasting.
m = (n·Σxy − Σx·Σy) / (n·Σx² − (Σx)²)
METHOD 04
NPV / IRR
Discounted cash flow Investment
Net Present Value discounts future cash flows to today's value. IRR finds the rate that makes NPV = 0, solved via Newton-Raphson on the NPV function itself.
NPV = Σ [ C_t / (1 + r)^t ] for t = 0..n
METHOD 05
Monte Carlo
Stochastic Risk modeling
Simulates 10,000 revenue paths by drawing random growth rates from N(μ, σ) at each period. Produces percentile bands showing the range of possible outcomes under uncertainty.
R_t = R_{t-1} · (1 + N(μ, σ))
